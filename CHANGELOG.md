# Changelog

Every orrery-node release in plain words, newest first. Times are when the release went up on npm. The same notes, easier on the eyes: [orrerynode.com/changelog](https://orrerynode.com/changelog).

Get the newest: `npx -y orrery-node@latest doctor`. `orrery doctor` also tells you when a newer release is out.

## Unreleased (in main)

- **Node** · Your books say what your holder prices gave. On each answer the node serves at its holder price, its log of answers now keeps the discount and what it came to: your list prices for the same tokens, less the holder price. `orrery provider earnings` says it under the totals ("holder prices: 12 of 59 answers went to $OREY holders at 35% off, $0.000412 under your list prices"), and so does the console under the last 24 hours; `/orrery/earnings` carries it as `holders`. The line the node closes each UTC day with, in its log and to your `notify` webhook, carries it too: "books for 2026-11-20 (UTC): 59 answers, $0.161250 billed (12 at your holder price, $0.004120 under list); …". It is the other side of the line your router keeps of what holding saved.
- **Router** · A node that is full is not a failed node. When a node with `slots` turns a request away as busy (every slot answering, nothing owed), your router now asks the next node without counting it against the full one, so its success rate and cooldown are untouched, and asks it after the others for 30 seconds instead of sending the next request to wait in the same queue. The log says so: "Lantern said every slot was answering; asking the next node, nothing counted against it, and it goes after the others for 30 s". The router’s console shows it in the node’s row ("full · asked after the others for 27 s"), and `/orrery/status` carries it as `full`, the seconds left.
- **Node** · `orrery provider earnings --json` gives a script your books: the rows by model or channel with their totals, the answers at your holder price and how far under list, the payouts in the window (count, amount, last transaction), how routers paid, and what the chain says has landed (`onChain`: payouts and settled). `--by day --json` and `--by month --json` give the books by day or month the same way. For a cron job, a spreadsheet or an alert, with nothing scraped from text.
- **Node** · Holder lanes. Set `"holderSlots": 1` next to `"slots"` and one of your slots only answers wallets that get your holder price. Everyone else shares the rest, so when your node is full for everyone else a holder still goes straight in, and the log says so ("holder request (−35%) went straight into a holder lane while 1 waited without a holder price"). `orrery provider status` and the console show the lanes ("2 of 2 answering (1 kept for holders), 1 waiting"). It needs `"slots"` and holder tiers, and always leaves at least one slot for everyone.
- **Router** · Your router says where you stand on holder prices, under its totals in the console and as `holder.line` in `/orrery/status`: your share of the $OREY supply, the holder price you get and at which nodes, and how much more of the supply gets you the nearest bigger discount ("You hold 0.5000% of $OREY's supply: 20% off at Halo Compute. 0.5000% more gets you 35% off there."). Read from the chain and the nodes' own price lists, on your machine. `orrery doctor` says the same under the router line ("holder price").
- **Everyone** · Holder-first models. A node can set `"holdersOnly": true` on a model in provider.json and open it to wallets that get its holder price only: a new model, a scarce one, or one it wants to keep for holders. The flag is in the price list on chain, so routers see it. A router whose wallet does not get that node's holder price never asks for it, and says so when that is the only way the model is served (403 `holders_only`); a request that reaches the node anyway is refused before anything is owed ("gemma-3-27b-early on this node is open to $OREY holders only, from 0.1% of the supply"). A node must list holder tiers to use it.
- **Router** · Your month as a card. The router's console draws this UTC month so far as an image you can save and post: what you paid, for how many answers, and what holding $OREY took off them, with a "Download PNG" button (2400×1260). It is made in your browser from the router's own log; nothing is uploaded. `/orrery/spend` carries the month's savings as `month.saved`.
- **Router** · Agent wallets. One router wallet, many agents, each with its own limit per UTC day: `orrery router --agent-cap researcher=0.50,coder=2` (or `ORRERY_AGENT_CAPS`). An agent names itself with the header `x-orrery-agent`, or with the API key `agent/<name>` for tools that only take a key. What the agent has left today bounds each of its requests, so the limit is never passed; each answer says `x-orrery-agent-spent` and `x-orrery-agent-left`, and once nothing is left the agent gets 402 until 00:00 UTC. The router's log names the agent on each answer and reads the day back after a restart, and `/orrery/status` lists every agent with its limit and what it spent today. An agent with no limit is only named, never held.
- **Router** · Each agent’s bill. `orrery spend --by agent` gives every named agent its answers, tokens and what it paid, with the answers that named no agent on a line of their own (`--csv` for a sheet, `/orrery/spend?by=agent` for the same rows), and your router’s console lists today’s agents with each limit, what each spent and what each has left, or that it is spent until 00:00 UTC.

## 0.1.24 · 2026-11-20 05:21 UTC

**$OREY holders go first on a busy node and see what holding saved them, every new node starts with holder prices, and payouts ping your webhook as they land.**

- **Node** · Every new node starts with holder prices: `orrery provider init` writes the seed node’s tiers into provider.json, so a wallet holding 0.1% of the network token’s supply pays 20% less and one holding 1% pays 35% less, and says so when it writes the file. `--no-holder-prices` leaves them out; the tiers are yours to edit before you register.
- **Router** · `orrery spend --by day` keeps your books of what your router paid: one row a UTC day (answers, tokens in and out, paid), newest first, a week unless `--since` says otherwise. `--csv` prints the rows alone for a spreadsheet or an expense report, by day, model, provider or task. It all comes from the router’s own log of answers on your disk. The router’s console shows the same books ("Paid by day (UTC), last 7 days") with a "Download 90 days as CSV" served by the router on your computer, and under them the month so far: "November so far (UTC): 2,104 answers, $1.082215 paid" (also the last line of `orrery spend --by day`).
- **Node** · Your node’s console says the month so far under its books by day: "November so far (UTC): 312 answers, $0.912500 billed; 14 payouts, $0.887250 paid out to your wallet", from the 1st of the UTC month, days kept in `days.jsonl` included. `/orrery/books` carries it as `month`.
- **Node** · `orrery provider earnings --by month` adds your books up by UTC calendar month (a year unless `--since` says otherwise): answers, billed, payouts and paid out for each month, newest first. With `--csv` it is one row a month, for an accountant. Months the logs no longer reach still count the days the node kept in `days.jsonl`.
- **Node** · Your node can tell you when money lands. Set `"notify": { "url": "https://hooks.slack.com/…" }` in provider.json (or `"urlEnv"` to read the address from an environment variable), or give it at the start with `orrery provider init --notify <url>` (`--notify-env <VAR>`), and each payout, once the node has read it back from the chain, is posted there as the same line its log says: "Kestrel GPU Co-op: $0.050123 arrived in your wallet: the chain’s record of tx 5GhKq2abcdef… shows it in your USDC account, which then held $1.412500". The JSON has it under `text` (what Slack’s incoming webhooks read) and `content` (what Discord’s read), with the figures under `payout` for a script. Just after midnight UTC the day’s books go there too, the line the log closes the day with, with the day’s totals under `books`. `orrery provider check` sends one test notice and says whether it got through, and `orrery provider status` and the console say where notices go and whether the last one did ("Payout notices go to hooks.slack.com; the last, a payout 4 min ago, got through (HTTP 200)"), naming the webhook’s host only.
- **Router** · Your router keeps what holding $OREY saved you. On each answer from a node that charged you its holder price, the router’s log of answers now records the discount and what it took off: the node’s list prices for the same tokens, less its holder prices for them. `orrery spend` (any `--by`) and the router’s console, under "Paid by day", say the total for the window: "Holding $OREY took $0.000412 off 12 of these answers: the nodes that served them charge holders 35% less." `/orrery/spend` and `orrery spend --json` carry it as `held`.
- **Node** · Holders go first when your node is busy. Set `"slots": 4` in provider.json and the node streams at most four answers from your model server at once. When all four are answering, the next request waits for a free slot, and a wallet that gets your holder price goes ahead of those that do not, the larger discount first, everyone else in the order they came. A request that waits 30 seconds is turned away as busy before anything is owed, so its router asks the next node. The log says when a holder went ahead ("holder request (−35%) took the next free slot ahead of 2 waiting without a holder price, after 0.6 s"), and `orrery provider status` says it under the other lines ("slots  4 of 4 answering, 2 waiting (1 a holder); holders went ahead 3 times since start"; `slots` in `--json`), and so does the node’s console, under its recent answers. Without `slots` nothing changes: every request goes straight to the model server.

`npx -y orrery-node@0.1.24`

## 0.1.23 · 2026-11-19 12:37 UTC

**Your node’s books go straight into a spreadsheet and outlast its logs, and the console says how much fee money is left for payouts.**

- **Node** · Your node’s books by day reach back past its rolled-over logs. When a UTC day closes, the node keeps that day’s totals (answers, billed, payouts, paid out) as one line in `days.jsonl`, which never rolls over, and `provider earnings --by day`, its CSV and the console’s books use it for any day the logs no longer reach. `payouts.jsonl` now rolls over at 10 MB instead of 1 MB, like the log of answers.
- **Node** · `orrery provider earnings --by day --csv` and `orrery provider payouts --csv` print your books as CSV for a spreadsheet or an accountant: one row a UTC day (answers, billed, payouts, paid out, in USDC), and one row a payout with its whole transaction, what the chain said reached your wallet and what the wallet then held. Nothing but the header and the rows, so it goes straight to a file. Your node’s console has the same as a download: "Download 90 days as CSV" beside its books by day, served by the node on this computer.
- **Node** · Your node’s console says how much fee money the node key has left for payouts, under the totals: "Fee money for payouts: 0.0123 SOL on the node key, about 2,460 payouts". Every payout is a transaction the node key pays for, so the line turns to a warning, with the address to send SOL to, before payouts would stop. It is read with the rest of the console’s chain reads, at most once a minute.

## 0.1.22 · 2026-11-19 05:56 UTC

**Your node’s books, checked against the chain: every payout read back and shown arriving in your wallet, what the wallet held after it, when the next payout goes out, and the day’s books in one line.**

- **Node** · Just after midnight UTC, your node says the day that ended in one line of its log: "books for 2026-11-18 (UTC): 1,312 answers, $0.038912 billed; 3 payouts, $0.120000 to your wallet; it held $1.412500 after the last, per the chain". It reads the files the node already keeps, so it asks the chain for nothing; a day with nothing served or paid out says nothing. `orrery provider earnings --by day` lays the same books out day by day (a week unless `--since` says otherwise): answers, billed, payouts and paid out for each UTC day, newest first, with the total under them. Your node’s console shows the last seven days the same way ("Your books by day (UTC)"), from `/orrery/books`.
- **Node** · An open console no longer makes your node read its listing from the chain every two seconds: what has landed on chain is read at most once a minute, and again right after the node sends a payout.
- **Node** · Your node’s console says when money next goes to your wallet, under the payouts: "$0.004211 is waiting in 1 channel. It pays out once it reaches $0.050000 or its router closes it; this node checks every 1 min." or "$0.061000 is due and goes out in the next round, within 1 min." It counts channel by channel, as the payout round does, so `orrery provider status` no longer adds two channels of $0.03 into one payout that is not coming: "($0.061000 at the next round, every 1 min; $0.060000 waits for $0.05 a channel)". `orrery provider earnings` ends with the same sentence when the node is running on that computer ("next payout: …"). `/orrery/status` carries it as `nextPayout`.
- **Node** · Your node watches its own payouts land. After each one it reads the transaction back from the chain and says in its log what reached your wallet: "$0.050123 arrived in your wallet: the chain’s record of tx 5GhKq2abcdef… shows it in your USDC account, which then held $1.250123" (the balance comes from the same record). A payout that failed on chain, or one that put in a different amount, is said too. The console’s "on chain" column fills in from there without pressing the button. One transaction lookup per payout, tried again a few seconds later if the RPC has no record yet.
- **Node** · `orrery provider payouts` checks your node’s books against the chain. It lists the payouts the node kept, newest first, and reads each one’s transaction from the chain to say what it put in your wallet’s USDC account: "2026-11-18 14:05:11 UTC  $0.050123  2 channels  tx 5GhKq2abcdef…  arrived $0.050123, wallet then $1.250123", then "5 of 5 arrived in your wallet as kept here, $0.240000 in all". A payout the chain has no record of, one that failed, or one that put in a different amount is named on its own line, and the command exits 1. `--last` sets how many it checks (5 by default, one transaction lookup each), `--json` gives the rows.
- **Node** · `orrery provider earnings` says, under what the window billed, what reached your wallet in the same window: "paid out: $0.120000 to your wallet in 2 payouts in the last 24h, the last 12 min ago (tx 5Gh4kQp9xTn2…)", from the payouts the node keeps. With `--since 7d` it covers the week, and when nothing went out in the window it says when the last payout was.
- **Node** · `orrery provider status` says whether anything outside your computer is reaching your node: "checked from outside  118 times in the last hour, the last 12 s ago". A router that knows a node asks its `/health` every 30 seconds, so this is the first sign your public address works, before any answer comes. Your node’s console says it under its heading, and the node says it in its own log too, where you watch `provider start`: "reached from outside 42 s after starting: the public address works", or, once, three minutes in with nothing, the two reasons and the command that tells which. When `provider start` checks its address before the host has switched traffic to the new process, it says that can take a minute and that the log will say when the node is reached. Your own `provider check` is not counted, and the node keeps only the count and the time, never who asked. `--json` and `/orrery/status` carry it as `reach`.
- **Router** · `orrery quote` and the `quote` tool of `orrery mcp` say, under the prices, what your own router saw of each node: "Ironbridge Compute  up in 80 of 82 checks this router made over 3 days; answered 16 of 23 requests this router sent it over 2 days; first token in about 960 ms lately; every bill was within its listed prices (41 answers in the last 24 h)". `orrery providers` says the bills too. A price and the record of who charges it are in one place, from your router’s own files, so nothing more is asked of the network. `/orrery/quote` and `--json` carry it as `saw`.
- **Node** · `orrery provider status` says where your listing stands on chain, judged by the price list your running node serves, since that is what routers read: "on chain, with this node’s prices and address"; "provider.json has new prices that are not live yet, and routers still get the listed ones"; or, after listing new prices without a restart, "this node still serves its old prices, so a router reading them now refuses it", each with the command that clears it. `--json` carries it as `listing`. `orrery provider check` now fetches `/v1/manifest` from your public address as a router does, and fails when what it serves is not the price list on chain.
- **Router** · The `embed` tool of `orrery mcp` does what `ask` does. An agent can put embedding calls under a task with `task` and `task_budget`, so a job that reads and writes draws from one budget, cap one call with `max_cost`, stay with one node with `node`, and set `patience`. Each result says which node answered and what the task has spent and has left, and when no node answered within the wait, `embed` asks once more.
- **Node** · `orrery provider check` says where each of your prices stands among the other listings of the same model, for the request the `:cheap` route compares (2,000 tokens in, 500 out): "llama-3.3-70b the cheapest of 2; gpt-oss-120b at $0.000145, the one other listing cheaper at $0.000140; deepseek-v3 listed by no other node", and, when some list a model cheaper, that `orrery provider price` suggests prices under them. It reads the listings a router on your cluster reads, and it is information: a node is just as ready to earn either way.
- **Node** · `orrery provider status` says what the last 24 hours billed, from the node’s own log of answers: "last 24 h  1,312 answers, $0.038912 billed". The count since start goes back to 0 when the node restarts; this one does not. `--json` carries it as `day`.
- **Node** · Your node’s console lists its payouts to your wallet: the latest 12, each with its time (UTC), what it paid out, how many channels it paid from and its transaction, and the last 24 hours under them. Its "Check against the chain" button reads each one’s transaction back from the chain and says beside it what reached your wallet ("arrived $0.050123"; a payout the chain has no record of, or one that put in a different amount, is marked), with a line under the table: "3 of 3 arrived in your wallet as kept here, $0.161250 in all". A payout found as kept is not looked up again while the node runs. Click a transaction to copy the whole signature and look it up on any Solana explorer; the console still names no other computer, so its address and key never leave this one. Beside the totals, the console shows what your wallet held right after the last payout, read from the chain’s record of that payout ("$1.412500 in your wallet, per the chain"); the node keeps it in `held.json` in its folder, `/orrery/status` carries it as `wallet`, and `orrery provider status` says it under the payouts ("in your wallet  $1.412500 after the last payout, per the chain (tx 5GhKq2abcdef…)"). A node that was paid out on chain before it began keeping its payouts says so in `orrery provider status` ("none recorded here yet; this listing was paid out on chain before") instead of "none yet".
- **Node** · `orrery provider status` says when money last landed in your wallet and what the last day came to: "last payout  $0.050123 to your wallet 12 min ago (14:05:11 UTC)  tx 5GhKq2abcdef…" and "paid out, last 24 h  $0.120000 in 3 payouts". The node keeps each payout it sends (when, how much, from how many channels, the transaction) in `payouts.jsonl` in its folder, so the lines survive a restart, and `/orrery/status` carries them as `payouts`. Before the first payout it says "none yet". Once the node has read the last payout back from the chain as it wrote it, the line ends "arrived, per the chain".

## 0.1.21 · 2026-11-18 01:12 UTC

**Your node lists the context its model server really serves, your router sends a long prompt only to a node that can take it and remembers every node across restarts, and a request no node answered is shown wherever you look as paid nothing.**

- **Router** · The `ask` and `embed` tools of `orrery mcp` wait up to 30 seconds for a node that is away, restarting say, before giving up, and `ask` asks once more when no node answered within that, since a restart can outlast one wait (the seed’s takes about 40 s), so an agent gets its answer instead of an error to retry. An agent that would rather fail at once passes `patience: 0`.
- **Router** · `orrery doctor` says what your router keeps on this computer, counted from its own files: "~/.orrery/router: 258 answers, 0 unanswered requests, a record of 1 node, 1 channel · models, token counts, prices and timings, never a prompt or an answer".
- **Node** · `orrery provider status` counts what your model server turned away in the last 24 hours, by kind: "model server refused  3 in the last 24 h: 2 longer than it serves, 1 busy". The node keeps each refusal (when, which model, which kind, never what the server said) in `refused.jsonl` in its folder, so the count survives a restart and you see it without reading the log. `orrery provider check` shows the same line when there were any.
- **Router** · A request no node answered, where every node your router asked failed and your client got a 502, is now kept in `~/.orrery/router/unanswered.jsonl`, and `orrery spend` says how many there were: "2 requests got no answer from any node asked (3 nodes asked in all); nothing was signed or paid for them." `/orrery/spend` carries the same counts as `unanswered`, the `status` tool of `orrery mcp` tells an agent the same line, `orrery doctor` adds it to its paid line, and your router’s console says it under its 24-hour spend.
- **Node** · `orrery provider init` on Ollama lists 4,096 tokens of context, with 1,024 for the answer, instead of 32,768. Ollama does not say in its model list how much context it serves, and it reads only as much of a prompt as its own setting allows and drops the rest without an error, so a larger listing drew long prompts it answered from their tail. init says how to raise both (start Ollama with `OLLAMA_CONTEXT_LENGTH` and set `context` to match). Ollama behind `--upstream` is known too, by its answer or its port.
- **Router** · `orrery providers` shows under each node what your own router saw of it, beside what the chain says: "what your router saw: up in 80 of 82 checks this router made over 3 days; answered 16 of 23 requests this router sent it over 2 days; first token in about 960 ms lately". It reads the records your router keeps in its home, so it asks nothing more of the network and works while the router is stopped.
- **Node** · `orrery provider check` fails a listing whose `maxOutput` is as large as its `context`: "qwen3-32b lists a maxOutput of 8,192 in a context of 8,192: nothing is left for the prompt, so a request that leaves max_tokens to the node fails at the model server. Set maxOutput to a quarter of context or less (2,048)". A request that does not set max_tokens gets the listing’s maxOutput, so with a listing like that every such request fails.
- **Router** · What your router counted of each node, the answers, the failures, the answers that broke its own listing and how fast it started, is kept in `~/.orrery/router/observed.json` and read back when the router starts, so a restart no longer forgets a node that failed it and trusts it afresh. A node not seen for 30 days is let go of, and a cooldown is not kept: it is for the moment.
- **Node** · When your model server turns a request away, your node says why in its own log, in one line you can act on: "the model server refused a deepseek-v3 request as longer than it serves (400); provider.json lists 131,072 tokens of context for it: set "context" to what the server serves", or that it does not know the model, refused the node’s key, or was busy. The server’s own words are never logged or passed on, since some servers quote the request back. A prompt longer than the server serves now answers routers `context_length_exceeded` (a 400), so a router moves on instead of waiting the node out as if it had gone away, and the router’s error names the node’s code.
- **Router** · A prompt clearly longer than every context on offer for its model is answered with the error your client already knows for that, so an agent shortens its conversation and goes on: 400 `context_length_exceeded` with "This model's maximum context length is 8192 tokens…" on the OpenAI APIs, "prompt is too long: 10000 tokens > 8192 maximum" on `/v1/messages`. No node is asked, so none is counted against. A request that lists fallback models moves on to the next one.
- **Router** · `orrery channels` says how much of each deposit is unspent, which is what closing the channel gives back, and adds it up on a last line: "Unspent: $1.999864 across 1 channel." For a channel your router has no record of, it says "up to", since the node may hold vouchers it has not redeemed yet.
- **Router** · A prompt clearly longer than the context a node lists goes to a node of the same model that lists enough, instead of to a node whose model server would refuse it, so the node passed over is not counted against in your router’s record. The prompt is measured from its text alone at five characters a token, a floor rather than an estimate, and when no node lists enough the request goes out as before.
- **Node** · `orrery provider init` lists the context your model server says it serves each model with (vLLM and SGLang report `max_model_len`), keeping a quarter of it, up to 4,096 tokens, for the answer. Before, every model was listed at 32,768 tokens, so a server started with `--max-model-len 8192` failed every longer request routers sent it, and those failures counted against the node. `orrery provider check` says when provider.json lists more context than the server serves.

## 0.1.19 · 2026-11-16 14:42 UTC

**Setting up a node can no longer cost you for a typo: `provider init` keeps the RPC you give it, a node is not listed at an address the internet cannot reach (unless you ask for that), and a channel deposit below the program’s minimum is explained, not a failed transaction.**

- **Node** · `orrery provider init --rpc <url>` (or `ORRERY_RPC_URL`) writes that RPC into `provider.json`. It wrote Solana’s public mainnet RPC whatever you gave it, so a node set up with your own RPC ran on the public one until you edited the file.
- **Node** · `orrery provider register` will not list a node on a public cluster under an address only your own network can reach (127.0.0.1, 192.168.x, 10.x, 172.16–31.x, a .local name and the like). ## 0.1.14 · 2026-11-12 04:02 UTC

## 0.1.13 · 2026-11-11 23:14 UTC

**Behind a proxy your router shows nobody where you are, any tool can carry a job’s budget in its API key, and every answer says where its time went.**

- **Router** · A node no longer has to learn your address, and neither does your RPC. `orrery router --proxy http://127.0.0.1:8118` (or `ORRERY_PROXY`) sends every request to a node through an HTTP proxy you choose, so the node sees the proxy’s address and not yours. For an https node the router asks the proxy for a tunnel and speaks TLS to the node inside it; requests are already encrypted to the node’s own key, so the proxy learns which node and how many bytes, and nothing of what was said. There is no fallback: with the proxy down a request fails, it does not go out directly, and a router will not start with a proxy that does not answer. The Solana RPC goes the same way: its calls are tunnelled too, and transactions are confirmed by asking for their status because the RPC’s websocket is never opened. So do the chip makers’ certificate lists. On mainnet, a router started this way held connections to the proxy and to nothing else. SOCKS is not spoken yet, and every request opens a new tunnel, so it is slower than going direct.
- **Router** · A job’s name and budget can ride in the API key. Most tools let you set a key and nothing else, so the router now reads `orrery/<task>/<budget>` from the key field (`Authorization: Bearer` or `x-api-key`) the way it reads the `x-orrery-task` headers. The router times five stages of every answer: choosing the node, making sure a channel is open, sealing the request, the wait for the node’s first event, and signing the payment. They come back in the standard `Server-Timing` header (`route`, `channel`, `seal`, `node`, `sign`) and show with the answer on `/orrery/status`. On mainnet the four stages on your own machine came to between 2.3 and 3.7 ms across five requests in a row; the first request after a start spent about 400 ms reading its channel once. A quote says which microcode and TDX module the platform runs; Intel publishes a signed list of which versions are current for each platform family. Your router fetches that list from Intel, checks its signature against the same pinned root, and refuses an enclave Intel lists as out of date, revoked or unsupported. `orrery enclave verify` prints it as its own line (`--allow-outdated` to look anyway, `--offline` to skip). On the sample quote we test with, it says what Intel says: older than every level Intel supports.
- **Router** · The AMD SEV-SNP verifier checks the firmware level. AMD publishes no signed list of current levels, so the floor is yours to set.
- **Router** · The router takes its enclave policy as flags. `orrery router --min-firmware 3.0.8.115` refuses AMD enclaves below a firmware level you choose (the environment variable still works; the flag wins), and `--allow-outdated` lets your router seal to an Intel enclave that Intel lists as out of date, which it refuses by default. A level that does not parse stops the router before it starts. `orrery help` now lists `--enclave-images` as well.
- **Router** · `orrery run` without `--budget` now gives the job a dollar. In 0.1.12 a run started without one had every request refused, because a job needs a budget from its first request; pass `--budget` there.
